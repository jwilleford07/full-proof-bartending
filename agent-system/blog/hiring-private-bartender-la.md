## What mobile bartending should include

“Mobile bartending” can mean different things depending on who you hire. For a premium private event, you generally want clarity on four layers—not just who pours drinks.

**Equipment and presentation.** A professional mobile bar brings the hardware needed to execute consistently: ice handling, mixing tools, glassware or disposable upgrade paths that match your event level, and a setup that fits your floor plan rather than fighting it.

**Service flow.** Good bar service matches your run-of-show: where guests arrive first, how traffic moves during peaks, and how you plan transitions such as speeches or toast timing.

**Execution standards.** Garnish discipline, pacing, and consistency matter more than flashy pours. Guests notice steadiness—especially hosts who are trying to stay present.

**Cleanup expectations.** Ask what wrap-down includes so there are no surprises when music stops.

## Questions worth asking any vendor

These questions protect both your timeline and your guest experience:

**Coverage and staffing.** How many bartenders do they recommend for your guest count and format? Who arrives for setup, and how early?

**Liquor licensing context (high level).** Requirements vary by venue type and arrangement. Ask how they plan within compliant hosting patterns so your planner or venue contact can validate quickly.

**Insurance and professionalism.** Confirm general liability coverage and any venue-required certificates without assuming—requirements differ.

**Run-of-show coordination.** How do they communicate with planners or day-of coordinators? What decisions need your approval before service?

**Ice and supplies.** Who sources ice and garnishes? How do they maintain temperature and hygiene during longer windows?

If answers feel vague or improvised, treat that as signal—not panic, just signal.

## Premium signals versus common red flags

**Signals that tend to correlate with professional execution**

- Written summaries after discovery calls or inquiry replies—guest range, hours, pour style, setup footprint.
- Specific staffing recommendations tied to your floor plan and cocktail concentration—not just “we’ll figure it out.”
- Straightforward discussion of ice sourcing, garnish prep windows, and glass service levels.

**Patterns worth a second look**

- Pricing that cannot explain what setup includes or assumes you already own half the bar infrastructure.
- Staffing promises that ignore realistic throughput math during cocktail-heavy arrivals.
- Cleanup described only as “we’ll tidy”—without confirming trash handling, breakdown timing, or venue-specific rules.

We avoid naming competitors here because your comparison should stay factual and calm—not noisy.

## A simple planning timeline

**Eight to twelve weeks out (typical peak seasons earlier):** Lock date and venue constraints; decide cocktail-heavy versus beer-and-wine-forward posture.

**Four to six weeks out:** Confirm guest band (even a range speeds staffing math); align toast or specialty pour timing.

**Two weeks out:** Final headcount drift; confirm access windows for setup and breakdown.

**One week out:** Freeze menu decisions affecting batch paths or garnish labor.

Earlier inquiries help secure availability on competitive Saturdays and holiday weekends across LA County and neighboring markets.
